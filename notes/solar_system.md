# The Solar System

## Overview
The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a giant cloud of gas and dust.
At its centre is the Sun, a star that contains 99.8% of all the mass in the Solar System.
Eight planets orbit the Sun. In order from the Sun they are: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars,
Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.

## Inner (rocky) planets
The four inner planets are small and made of rock and metal. They are called terrestrial planets.

- **Mercury** is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has almost no atmosphere,
  so its temperature swings from 430°C in the day to -180°C at night. A year on Mercury lasts 88 Earth days.
- **Venus** is the hottest planet, with surface temperatures around 465°C, even though it is
  further from the Sun than Mercury. Its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat (a runaway
  greenhouse effect). Venus spins backwards compared to most planets.
- **Earth** is the only planet known to support life. About 71% of its surface is covered by water.
  It has one natural satellite, the Moon.
- **Mars** is called the Red Planet because of iron oxide (rust) on its surface. It has the tallest
  volcano in the Solar System, Olympus Mons, which is about 22 km high. Mars has two small moons,
  Phobos and Deimos.

## The asteroid belt
Between Mars and Jupiter lies the asteroid belt, a region filled with rocky objects.
The largest object in the belt is Ceres, which is classified as a dwarf planet.

## Outer planets (giants)
- **Jupiter** is the largest planet. It is a gas giant made mostly of hydrogen and helium and is about
  11 times wider than Earth. Its Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has lasted for
  hundreds of years. Jupiter has at least 95 known moons; the largest, Ganymede, is bigger than Mercury.
- **Saturn** is famous for its bright rings made of ice and rock. It is the least dense planet:
  it would float in water if a big enough bathtub existed.
- **Uranus** is an ice giant that rotates on its side, with an axial tilt of about 98 degrees.
- **Neptune** is the farthest planet from the Sun. It has the fastest winds in the Solar System,
  reaching over 2,000 km/h. One year on Neptune lasts about 165 Earth years.

## Dwarf planets
Pluto was considered the ninth planet until 2006, when the International Astronomical Union
reclassified it as a dwarf planet. A dwarf planet orbits the Sun and is round, but has not
cleared other objects from its orbit.
